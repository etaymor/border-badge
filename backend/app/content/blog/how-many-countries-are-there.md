Ask three travelers how many countries there are and you will get three integers. None of them is making it up. They are using different lists.

This matters the moment you open a tracking app. Been talks about 250+. Country Count uses 195. Atlasi tracks **227 countries and territories**. Your personal total will jump or drop when you switch, even if you have not left the sofa. The [apps to track countries visited](/blog/best-apps-to-track-countries-visited) all inherit this disagreement. So does the separate question of [whether a layover counts](/blog/does-a-layover-count-as-visiting-a-country).

Here is the map of the numbers, and why Atlasi lands on 227.

## The Counts, Side by Side

| Count | What it includes | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **193** | UN member states only | Formal writing, UN documents |
| **195** | 193 plus the two observer states (Holy See, Palestine) | Quizzes, most "how many countries" answers, many scratch maps |
| **197** | 195 plus two extras — usually Taiwan and Kosovo, sometimes Cook Islands and Niue instead | Travel lists that want widely visited places the UN roll omits |
| **~250** | Sovereign states plus a large set of territories and dependencies | Been (250+), some ISO-adjacent datasets |
| **227** | UN members, observers, disputed states, dependent territories, constituent countries, and special regions | **Atlasi** |

No count is "the real one." A number is only comparable if you know which row produced it.

## 193 and 195: The UN Baseline

The United Nations has **193 member states**. South Sudan was the last to join, in 2011. That is the tightest defensible figure.

**195** adds the two non-member observer states:

*   The **Holy See**, which governs Vatican City and has never applied for full membership.
*   **Palestine**, granted non-member observer status in 2012.

This is the number most people mean when they say "countries in the world" without a footnote. It is also why two friends can argue past each other: one is counting Vatican City, the other is not.

## 197: The Travel Argument

Some lists add **Taiwan** and **Kosovo**, which large numbers of travelers visit and which are not UN members. Other 197-lists add **Cook Islands** and **Niue** (states in free association with New Zealand) instead.

The honest move is to name the extras. "197" without a footnote is how people talk past each other.

Atlasi includes Taiwan, Kosovo, and Northern Cyprus on the 227 list as disputed / separately tracked places, because folding them into a neighbor erases trips people actually took.

## Why Atlasi Uses 227

Atlasi's passport grid is not trying to settle international law. It is trying to match how travel *feels*. A week in the Faroe Islands is not "Denmark, again." A trip to French Polynesia is not the same stamp as Paris.

The 227 list is built from:

*   UN member states
*   UN observers (Vatican City, Palestine)
*   Disputed territories (including Kosovo, Taiwan, Northern Cyprus)
*   Dependent territories
*   Special regions (Hong Kong, Macau, Antarctica)
*   Constituent countries where travelers treat them as their own stamp

That is why remote territories count toward your total rather than disappearing into a parent country. It is also why your Atlasi number will usually be *higher* than a 195-based app for the same passport, if you have spent time in territories.

Been's 250+ list goes even further into territories. Maprat publishes 256. None of these are wrong. They are longer menus.

## How to Talk About Your Number

1. **Pick a list once.** Switching from 195 to 227 is not "gaining countries." It is changing the denominator.
2. **Say the denominator.** "52 of 227" is information. "52 countries" is a vibe.
3. **Do not mix layover rules with list rules.** Whether an airside transit counts is a separate decision, covered in [does a layover count as visiting a country](/blog/does-a-layover-count-as-visiting-a-country).
4. **If you only want the tally,** a simple counter is enough — Been on 250+, or any 195 app. **If you want the territories you actually visited to show up as themselves,** use a 227-style grid. That is what **[Atlasi]({{APP_STORE_URL}})** is for.

Country counts change slowly (new members are rare) and arguments about them do not. The useful habit is consistency, not winning the geography thread.
