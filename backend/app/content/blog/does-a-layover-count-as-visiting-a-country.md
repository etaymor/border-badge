There is no ICAO officer who will audit your country count. There is only you, a map, and the friend who says "that does not count" when you mention a 90-minute connection in Istanbul.

The argument is older than apps. Passport-stamp collectors, UN-list purists, and "I only count overnights" travelers have been talking past each other for decades. A tracking app does not resolve it. It just makes the number public.

This is a practical rule set you can apply in any tracker, including the [apps to track countries visited](/blog/best-apps-to-track-countries-visited). It is separate from [how many countries exist](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there) — that is a list problem. This is a *visit* problem.

## Pick a Rule, Then Freeze It

None of these is morally superior. Inconsistency is the only real failure: counting a 2019 Singapore landside lunch and skipping a 2024 Singapore landside lunch because you are being stricter now.

Write the rule in one sentence and keep it.

| Rule | Counts as a visit | Does not count | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Airside-no** | You cleared immigration or left the sterile transit zone | Gate-to-gate connections, flyovers | Most travelers, easiest to defend |
| **Stamp-only** | A passport stamp or equivalent entry record | Airside transits, some land borders that never stamp | People who already think in stamps |
| **Overnight** | A night on the ground outside the airport | Same-day connections, day trips, some long layovers | People who want a high bar |
| **Anything-goes** | Any GPS ping, including the runway | Almost nothing | Photo apps left on autopilot |

Atlasi users should use **airside-no**. It matches how Photo Import works: you confirm places, so an airport cluster can be skipped without drama.

## The Cases That Cause Fights

### The long layover you never left
Nine hours in Hamad International, a lounge, a shower, no immigration. **Airside-no: no.** You were in Qatar's airport. You were not in Qatar in the sense anyone describing a trip would mean.

### The layover where you took the free city tour
You cleared, you saw a mosque, you went back. **Airside-no: yes.** That is a short visit, not a connection.

### The overnight in an airside hotel
Some airports let you sleep without entering. **Airside-no: no.** **Overnight (city): no.** **Stamp-only:** only if they stamped you.

### The cruise port
You went ashore for four hours. **Yes**, under airside-no and stamp-only (if they stamped). Overnight-only people will skip it. Say so if you use that bar.

### The train or car that crossed a line
You bought coffee on the platform. **Yes.** You slept through the border in a couchette. **Airside-no says no**; overnight-only also says no unless you got off.

### The flyover and the window photo
**Never.** Photo-based apps are sloppy here. If you use [apps that track countries from photos](/blog/best-apps-that-track-countries-from-photos), treat autopilot suggestions as a draft.

### Childhood trips you barely remember
Decide once whether "I was six and we changed planes" belongs. Put the decision next to the country, not in a yearly mood.

## How to Apply This in an App

**Manual maps (Been, Skratch, scratch-off apps):** nothing stops you tapping a country you only transited. The honor system *is* the product.

**Photo import (Atlasi and the camera-roll trackers):** the GPS does not know if you cleared immigration. A cluster at FRA might be a 50-minute connection. In Atlasi, do not confirm airport-only suggestions. If you did go into town, confirm the place, not just the country.

**Atlasi specifically:** stamp the country when it meets your rule. Log the lounge or city stop if you have one. Childhood countries without photos can still be stamped on the passport grid by hand. Photo Import is optional; it should not become an accidental layover vacuum.

## A Sentence You Can Reuse

> I count a country when I have cleared the border and spent time on the ground. Airport-only connections and flyovers do not count. I use the same rule for every year.

That sentence will not satisfy the overnight-only crowd. It will survive a dinner conversation.
