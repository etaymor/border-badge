There is a unique satisfaction in looking at a world map and seeing all the places you've explored. Whether you're trying to visit every country in the world or just want to keep a record of your summer vacations, having a dedicated app to track where you've been is worth setting up.

But the "best" app depends on what you want to do with that information. Do you just want a simple percentage score? Do you want to track specific regions? Or do you want to save specific restaurants and hotels within those countries to share with friends? If you want the broader category, start with our roundup of the [best travel tracking apps of 2026](/blog/best-travel-tracking-apps-2026).

We compared the publicly documented features and pricing of the top options. Here are the **5 best apps to track countries visited in 2026**.

## 1. Been: The Best for Minimalist Country Counting

If your only goal is to answer the question, "How many countries have I visited?", the **Been** app is hard to beat. It is the most minimalist country-counting app available.

### Why We Love It
*   **Simplicity**: The interface is straightforward. You scroll through a list of continents and check off the countries you've been to. That's it.
*   **Data Visualization**: It calculates what percentage of the world, and what percentage of each continent, you have explored.
*   **3D Globe**: The interactive 3D globe feature is a fun way to visualize your global footprint.

### The Drawbacks
Been is *only* a country counter. You cannot log specific cities, save your favorite restaurants, or write journal entries. If you want to remember *what* you did in a country, Been won't help you.

---

## 2. Atlasi: The Best for Tracking Specific Places and Recommendations

While checking off a whole country is satisfying, travel is really about the specific places you discover—that pasta spot in Rome, the hidden beach in Bali, or the boutique hotel in Kyoto. If you want to track countries *and* the specific places within them, **Atlasi** is the better fit.

### Why We Love It
*   **Granular Tracking**: Instead of just saying "I went to Italy," you log the specific places you visited, categorized by Food, Stay, Place, or Experience.
*   **Google Places Integration**: When you log a spot, Atlasi automatically pulls in the address and details via Google Places.
*   **Shareable Lists**: You can bundle your entries into [curated, shareable travel lists](/blog/best-apps-for-travel-lists) (e.g., "My 2-Week Japan Itinerary") and send them to friends who ask for recommendations.
*   **Low Battery Impact**: Because it relies on active logging rather than passive GPS tracking, it won't run down your battery while you travel.

### The Drawbacks
If you truly *only* want a simple checklist of countries without adding any specific details, Atlasi has more features than you need — Been is the cleaner choice. Atlasi is also iOS-only today.

---

## 3. Skratch: The Best for a Gamified Experience

If you've ever owned one of those physical scratch-off maps you hang on a wall, **Skratch** is the digital equivalent. It turns tracking your travels into a highly visual, gamified experience.

### Why We Love It
*   **The Aesthetic**: The interface lets you "scratch off" the countries you've visited, revealing a colorful map underneath.
*   **Regional Tracking**: Skratch allows you to track your travels down to the state, province, or regional level, which helps in large countries like the US or Canada.
*   **Video Pins**: You can pin short videos to specific locations on your map to bring your memories to life.

### The Drawbacks
Several features, including the detailed regional maps, require a premium subscription.

---

## 4. Visited: The Best for Goal-Oriented Travelers

The **Visited** app is designed for travelers who are motivated by stats, bucket lists, and seeing how they stack up against others.

### Why We Love It
*   **Extensive Bucket Lists**: You can track your progress against famous lists, such as the "Wonders of the World," "Top Art Museums," or "Best Beaches."
*   **Travel Stats**: The app provides detailed analytics about your travel style and shows you how your country count compares to other users.
*   **Future Planning**: It places a heavy emphasis on tracking where you *want* to go, not just where you've been.

### The Drawbacks
The interface presents a lot of lists, stats, and upsells at once, so it lacks the simplicity of Been or Atlasi.

---

## 5. Mark O'Travel: The Best Free Regional Tracker

Mark O'Travel is a lesser-known but effective app for travelers who want detailed regional tracking without paying for a premium subscription.

### Why We Love It
*   **Deep Regional Data**: It breaks countries into specific regions (e.g., tracking individual counties in the UK or prefectures in Japan).
*   **Custom Map Colors**: You can customize the colors of your map to fit your personal aesthetic.
*   **Free Regional Tracking**: Unlike Skratch, the regional tracking features are available at no cost.

### The Drawbacks
The user interface feels dated compared to newer apps, and it lacks social or sharing features.
