Remember when "tracking your travels" meant sticking a physical pin into a corkboard map? In 2026, the best travel tracking apps do far more than just log your location. They are digital journals, trip planners, social networks, and recommendation engines all rolled into one.

But with so many options on the App Store, choosing the right one can be overwhelming. Some apps focus on passively tracking your GPS route, while others focus on curating lists of your favorite restaurants.

To help you decide, we compared the publicly documented features and pricing of the **5 best travel tracking apps of 2026**. Here is our guide.

_Atlasi is our app. We've tried to be straight about what it does and doesn't do — the comparison table below includes where it loses._

| App | Best Feature | Main Drawback | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlasi** | Curated recommendation lists | No passive GPS tracking | Sharing favorite spots |

## 1. Atlasi: Best for Curating and Sharing Recommendations

If you're the person in your friend group who always gets asked, "Hey, what was that amazing sushi place you went to in Tokyo?"—**Atlasi** is the app for you.

Most travel tracking apps focus entirely on *you* and your route. Atlasi focuses on the *places* you visited, making it easy to save, categorize, and share your favorite discoveries.

### Why We Love It
*   **Smart Categories**: Instead of just dropping a pin, you log entries specifically as Food, Stay, Place, or Experience.
*   **Shareable Lists**: You can bundle your entries into shareable lists (e.g., "The Ultimate Kyoto Food Guide" or "Best Cheap Stays in Europe"). If that's your main use case, it's worth reading our roundup of [apps for creating and sharing travel lists](/blog/best-apps-for-travel-lists).
*   **Google Places Integration**: When you log a restaurant, Atlasi automatically pulls in the correct address, hours, and contact info via Google Places.
*   **Privacy Controls**: Keep your lists private by default, or publish a list to a public link you choose to share.

**The Verdict**: Atlasi is the best travel tracking app for people who want their travel data to be useful—not just for their own memories, but for helping others plan their trips.

---

## 2. PolarSteps: Best for Visual Route Mapping

PolarSteps remains one of the most popular travel tracking apps on the market, and for good reason. It excels at doing one specific thing: drawing a line on a map that shows exactly where you went.

### Why We Love It
*   **Passive Tracking**: Turn it on, put your phone in your pocket, and PolarSteps tracks your route automatically using your phone's GPS.
*   **Travel Books**: At the end of your trip, the app can generate a physical, orderable photo book containing your route, photos, and notes.
*   **Map Visualization**: The route map is genuinely nice to look at, and it's satisfying to watch it grow over time.

**The Drawbacks**: Continuous GPS tracking will drain your battery faster than an app that only reads location on demand. It's also not built for sharing specific recommendations; it's designed as a personal journal. If that's a dealbreaker, we've written up [six PolarSteps alternatives](/blog/best-polarsteps-alternatives) and a full [Atlasi vs. PolarSteps head-to-head](/blog/atlasi-vs-polarsteps).

**The Verdict**: PolarSteps is ideal for backpackers and road-trippers who want a visual map of their journey and a physical souvenir at the end.

---

## 3. Been: Best for Minimalist Country Counting

Sometimes, less is more. The **Been** app doesn't track your route, and it doesn't let you write detailed journal entries. It does one thing: it lets you check off the countries you've visited.

### Why We Love It
*   **Simple**: You can set up the app and log your entire travel history in a few minutes.
*   **Data Visualization**: It tells you what percentage of the world (and specific continents) you have explored.
*   **3D Globe**: Viewing your travels on the interactive 3D globe is surprisingly addictive.

**The Drawbacks**: It's almost *too* simple. Once you've checked off your countries, there isn't much reason to open the app again until your next international trip.

**The Verdict**: Been is a good fit for travelers who just want a digital checklist. For a wider look at this category, see our comparison of [apps to track countries visited](/blog/best-apps-to-track-countries-visited).

## 4. Skratch: Best for Gamified Tracking

If you love the aesthetic of those physical scratch-off maps, **Skratch** is the digital equivalent. It's a highly visual app that makes tracking your travels feel like a game.

### Why We Love It
*   **Digital Scratch Map**: The interface lets you "scratch off" the countries you've visited, revealing the map underneath.
*   **Regional Tracking**: Unlike Been, Skratch tracks your travels down to the state, province, or regional level.
*   **Video Integration**: You can pin short videos to specific locations on your map.

**The Drawbacks**: Several of the more detailed features, including regional maps, sit behind a premium subscription.

**The Verdict**: Skratch is a fun, highly visual alternative for travelers who want to track regions, not just countries, and enjoy a gamified experience.

---

## 5. Visited: Best for Goal-Oriented Travelers

The **Visited** app is designed for travelers who are motivated by stats, lists, and goals. It tracks where you've been, but it places equal emphasis on where you *want* to go.

### Why We Love It
*   **Extensive Lists**: Track your progress against famous lists (e.g., "Wonders of the World," "Top Art Museums," or "Best Beaches").
*   **Travel Stats**: Provides detailed analytics about your travel style and how you compare to other users.
*   **Goal Setting**: Easily build out a comprehensive bucket list and track your progress toward completing it.

**The Drawbacks**: The interface packs in a lot of stats and lists at once, which feels dense next to the cleaner design of Atlasi or Been.

**The Verdict**: Visited is the best app for competitive travelers who love checking items off a bucket list and tracking their stats.
