PolarSteps has been a go-to travel tracking app for years, helping backpackers and vacationers plot their routes on a map. But as travel has evolved, so have the apps we use to document it.

While PolarSteps is great for passively tracking your GPS location and generating a photo book, many travelers are looking for something more. Maybe you want to share specific restaurant recommendations, build curated lists of your favorite spots, or avoid the battery drain of constant GPS tracking.

If you're looking for an app that does more than just draw a line on a map, you're in the right place. We compared the publicly documented features and pricing of the **6 best PolarSteps alternatives for 2026** to help you find the right companion for your next adventure. ## 1. Atlasi: The Best Alternative for Sharing Recommendations

If your main frustration with PolarSteps is that it's hard to share *specific* recommendations (like that hidden taco stand in Mexico City or the boutique hotel in Kyoto), **Atlasi** is the alternative you've been looking for.

While PolarSteps focuses on the route, Atlasi focuses on the *places*. It allows you to log specific entries—categorized into Food, Stay, Place, and Experience—and compile them into shareable lists.

### Key Features
*   **Categorized Entries**: Tag your spots as Food, Stay, Place, or Experience.
*   **Curated Lists**: Create and share custom lists (e.g., "Best Coffee Shops in Rome" or "My 2-Week Japan Itinerary").
*   **Google Places Integration**: Automatically pulls in accurate location data, hours, and contact info.
*   **Interactive Map**: See all your saved places on a global map.

### Pros
*   Well suited to travelers who like giving recommendations to friends.
*   No passive GPS tracking, which keeps battery impact low.
*   Clean, modern interface.
*   Makes your travel data useful for future planning.

### Cons
*   Doesn't passively track your exact route step-by-step.
*   Newer app, so the community is still growing.
*   iPhone and iPad only today; Android is in development.

**Verdict**: Choose Atlasi if you want to turn your travel memories into actionable recommendations and curated lists to share with friends. We've also written a direct [Atlasi vs. PolarSteps comparison](/blog/atlasi-vs-polarsteps) if those two are your shortlist.

## 2. Wanderlog: The Best Alternative for Detailed Planners

Wanderlog is less of a travel tracker and more of a comprehensive trip planner. If you felt PolarSteps lacked the ability to actually *plan* your trip before you go, Wanderlog fills that gap.

### Key Features
*   **Drag-and-Drop Itinerary**: Plan your days down to the hour.
*   **Route Optimization**: Automatically calculates an efficient route between stops.
*   **Expense Tracking**: Keep a running tally of your trip costs.

### Pros
*   Strong integration with Google Maps.
*   Good collaborative features for group trips.
*   Pulls reservations directly from your email.

### Cons
*   Can be a lot of structure for travelers who prefer spontaneity.
*   Some features, including offline access, sit behind a $49.99/year plan.

**Verdict**: Choose Wanderlog if you want every detail organized before you board the plane.

## 3. Been: The Best Alternative for Simple Country Counting

Sometimes you don't need a detailed map of every step you took; you just want to know what percentage of the world you've seen. For country counting, the **Been** app is the minimalist alternative to PolarSteps.

### Key Features
*   **Country Checklists**: Simply check off the countries and US states you've visited.
*   **Data Visualization**: See the percentage of the world and specific continents you've explored.
*   **3D Globe**: View your travels on an interactive 3D globe.

### Pros
*   Simple and satisfying to use.
*   No passive GPS tracking, so no meaningful battery cost.
*   Good visual representation of your global footprint.

### Cons
*   Tracks countries and states only, not specific cities or places.
*   No ability to add photos or notes.

**Verdict**: Choose Been if your main goal is to color in the map and track your country count. Our guide to [apps to track countries visited](/blog/best-apps-to-track-countries-visited) covers this category in more depth.

## 4. Skratch: The Best Alternative for Visualizing Your History

Skratch takes the concept of the physical "scratch-off map" you might have on your wall and digitizes it. It's a good PolarSteps alternative for travelers who want a highly visual, gamified way to look at their travel history.

### Key Features
*   **Digital Scratch Map**: "Scratch off" countries and regions as you visit them.
*   **Region-Level Tracking**: Tracks down to the state/province level, not just countries.
*   **Video Integration**: Allows you to pin short videos to specific locations.

### Pros
*   Engaging, gamified interface.
*   More granular than the Been app (tracks regions, not just countries).
*   Allows for media uploads to bring the map to life.

### Cons
*   Several features require a subscription.
*   Not built for planning future trips.

**Verdict**: Choose Skratch if you love the aesthetic of scratch-off maps and want a fun way to visualize your past travels.

## 5. FindPenguins: The Closest Direct Competitor

If you actually *like* the core functionality of PolarSteps (passive GPS tracking and automatic photo books) but want a different flavor, FindPenguins is the closest direct alternative on the market.

### Key Features
*   **"Footprints"**: Automatically tracks your route and creates "footprints" (stops) along the way.
*   **Offline Tracking**: Works offline, syncing when you reconnect.
*   **Travel Books**: Generates orderable photo books.

### Pros
*   Strong offline capabilities for remote travel.
*   Supports collaborative tracking for group trips.
*   Accurate passive tracking.

### Cons
*   The interface feels dated compared to newer apps.
*   Passive tracking will drain your battery, just like PolarSteps.

**Verdict**: Choose FindPenguins if you want what PolarSteps offers, but with better support for group travel and offline tracking. If it's the journaling side you care about, compare the options in our guide to the [best travel journal apps](/blog/best-travel-journal-apps).

## 6. TripIt: The Best Alternative for Business Travelers

TripIt approaches travel tracking from a completely different angle. Instead of tracking your GPS, it tracks your *inbox*. It's an organizational tool for frequent flyers and business travelers.

### Key Features
*   **Master Itinerary**: Forward your confirmation emails to TripIt, and it builds a master itinerary automatically.
*   **Document Storage**: Keeps all your PDFs, QR codes, and booking numbers in one place.
*   **Flight Alerts**: Real-time notifications for delays and gate changes (Pro version).

### Pros
*   Requires almost zero manual data entry.
*   Reliable for keeping track of complex travel logistics.
*   The Pro version is useful for frequent flyers.

### Cons
*   Purely utilitarian rather than fun.
*   Doesn't track your route on a map or serve as a travel journal.

**Verdict**: Choose TripIt if your main goal is organizing your booking confirmations rather than journaling your experiences.
