For years, tracking your travels meant carrying a physical journal or uploading a massive photo dump to Facebook after you got home. Today, apps like PolarSteps and Atlasi have changed how we document our journeys.

If you're looking for an app to log your trips, you've likely come across both of these options. While they are both "travel tracking apps," they serve very different purposes and cater to different types of travelers. ## The Short Version: How Do They Differ?

The fundamental difference between Atlasi and PolarSteps comes down to **Routes vs. Recommendations**.

*   **PolarSteps** is designed to passively track your GPS route. It's a personal journal that draws a line on a map showing exactly where you went.
*   **Atlasi** is designed to curate and share specific places. It's a recommendation engine that lets you log specific restaurants, hotels, and experiences to share with friends.

If you want a physical photo book of your road trip, choose PolarSteps. If you want to build a shareable list of the best coffee shops in Paris, choose Atlasi.

## Deep Dive: PolarSteps Review

PolarSteps, founded in Amsterdam in 2015, is one of the most widely used travel tracking apps on the market.

### How PolarSteps Works
The core feature of PolarSteps is its passive GPS tracking. Once you start a trip, the app runs in the background, pinging your location and drawing a continuous line on a map. As you travel, you can manually add "Steps"—photos and short journal entries—to specific points along your route.

At the end of your trip, PolarSteps can generate a physical "Travel Book" that you can purchase.

### Where PolarSteps Shines
*   **Zero Effort Tracking**: Because it tracks your GPS automatically, you don't have to remember to log your location.
*   **The Travel Books**: The printed photo books are well made and work as souvenirs or gifts.
*   **Visual Mapping**: The interface is polished, and watching your route snake across the globe is satisfying.

### Where PolarSteps Falls Short
*   **Battery Drain**: Continuous GPS tracking uses meaningfully more battery than on-demand location, which matters on a long travel day.
*   **Not Great for Recommendations**: If a friend asks for your favorite restaurants in Rome, PolarSteps isn't very helpful. Your "Steps" are tied to a chronological route, not categorized by type.
*   **Limited Group Features**: You cannot collaborate on a single trip with multiple travelers; everyone tracks their own route separately. If that's your sticking point, we've rounded up [six PolarSteps alternatives](/blog/best-polarsteps-alternatives), several of which do support group trips.

## Deep Dive: Atlasi Review

Atlasi is a newer entrant to the travel tracking space, built specifically to solve the "recommendation problem" that route-first apps don't address.

### How Atlasi Works
Instead of passively tracking your route, Atlasi relies on active logging. When you visit a place you love, you log it as an entry. Every entry is categorized as **Food, Stay, Place, or Experience**.

Because Atlasi integrates directly with Google Places, your entries automatically pull in accurate location data, opening hours, and contact information. You can then bundle these entries into shareable, curated lists.

### Where Atlasi Shines
*   **Shareable Lists**: Want to send your friend a list of "The 5 Best Tacos in CDMX"? Atlasi makes this straightforward, and every published list gets a public link that opens in any browser. We compare this category in our guide to [apps for creating and sharing travel lists](/blog/best-apps-for-travel-lists).
*   **Useful Data**: Because entries are categorized and linked to Google Places, your travel data becomes actionable for future planning.
*   **Low Battery Impact**: Atlasi only uses your location when you actively log an entry.
*   **Privacy Controls**: Trips and entries are private by default; you choose what to publish.

### Where Atlasi Falls Short
*   **No Passive Tracking**: If you want a detailed, step-by-step map of your road trip route, Atlasi won't draw that line for you.
*   **Active Logging Required**: You have to remember to log the places you visit; the app won't do it automatically.
*   **iOS Only**: Atlasi is on iPhone and iPad today, with Android in development.

## The Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

Both Atlasi and PolarSteps are solid apps, but they serve different needs.

**Choose PolarSteps if:**
*   You are going on a long road trip or backpacking journey and want to see the exact route you took.
*   You want a physical, printed photo book of your travels.
*   You prefer a passive "set it and forget it" tracking experience.

**Choose Atlasi if:**
*   You love discovering great restaurants, boutique hotels, and unique experiences.
*   You frequently share travel recommendations with friends and family.
*   You want to build curated lists (e.g., "My Tokyo Itinerary") that are actually useful for planning.
*   You want to keep battery impact low while traveling.
